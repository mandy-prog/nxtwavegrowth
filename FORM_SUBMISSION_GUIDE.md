### Field 6: `What did AI suggest you while building this that you deliberately rejected and why?`
Copy and paste this exact response:

```text
While building both the distribution plan and the working product, I deliberately rejected three major AI suggestions where generic LLM advice conflicted with real-world Indian engineering college behavior:

1. Rejected AI's Paid Ad Budget Split (₹1,200 Meta Ads + ₹500 LinkedIn Ads):
When I gave AI the ₹2,000 budget and 7-day constraint, it immediately suggested running Instagram/Facebook lead ads and LinkedIn sponsored messages. I rejected this completely. LinkedIn has a ~₹800/day minimum spend and high CPMs, while cold EdTech webinar ads on Meta take 48+ hours in the learning phase and cost ₹45–₹75 per student signup (meaning ₹2,000 would barely get ~35 registrations). Instead, I reallocated ₹1,200 into 6 performance micro-bounties (₹300/₹200/₹100) for 25 final-year Class Representatives (CRs) and ₹500 into 5 college coding club co-hosted sprints. A message posted by a Class CR inside an official final-year placement WhatsApp group carries peer authority, costs ~₹5.21 per signup, and gets read within 15 minutes.

2. Rejected "Certificate of Completion" Positioning & an 8-Field Registration Form:
For the landing page, AI generated generic hype copy ("Master the Future of AI in 60 Minutes & Earn an Exclusive Certificate!") alongside an 8-field form asking for Email, CGPA, LinkedIn URL, and GitHub URL. I rejected both. Final-year B.Tech students already have dozens of useless webinar certificates; what actually keeps them awake is "Resume Shame" (having generic copy-pasted Titanic/Iris projects during placement season). I rewrote the hook around replacing toy resume projects and built an interactive "Resume Bullet Previewer" so students can see the exact STAR-formatted resume bullet they will earn before they even sign up. I also cut the form down to 4 mobile-friendly fields + WhatsApp number since students open class links on their phones between lectures.

3. Rejected Leaderboard-Only Referrals & Post-Workshop ZIP Code Uploads:
For the working asset, AI suggested a "Top 3 referrers win vouchers" contest and having students upload ZIP files of their code after the workshop. I rejected the top-3-only referral model because 98% of students know they won't beat campus influencers and don't even try; instead, I built a guaranteed 2-friend "Study-Squad" unlock (referring just 2 lab partners unlocks the ATS Resume Prompt Pack immediately). Finally, because 500 students won't zip files at 8 PM on a Sunday, I built an in-browser Automated AI Rubric Evaluator (Tab 4) where students paste their Colab link + system prompt at Minute 52 to get an instant score out of 100 and a shareable LinkedIn Proof-of-Build card.
```
